## Notes - Idea

- were sure to make any kind of website -> 3D printing service was our final idea as website
- The platform is a flexible marketplace supporting two main use cases: purchasing ready-made 3D-printed products or downloading models for independent printing.
- This dual approach targets both non-technical users seeking ready-to-use products and experienced users with 3D printers who prefer self-printing for cost efficiency.
- The project emphasizes usability, accessibility, and clear interface design to provide an intuitive shopping experience for a broad audience.

- tried to legitimize every UI element by always giving a reason / explanation for their usage / changes

- immediately apply best practices from the lecture like laws for design or metrics to evaluate our UI for user-friendlyness and usability
  - also include friends and other people in the process and apply "user-driven" development and ask for immediate feedback

## Notes - Prototype (Figma)

### Initial (Paper) Sketches

- started with paper sketches to quickly iterate on the design and layout of the website
- defined the different slided and their content (e.g. homepage, product page, checkout process, etc.)
- created the initial design for the homepage
  - what colors to use throughout the website (e.g. for buttons, background, etc.)
  - how to structure the content on the homepage (e.g. categories, featured products, etc.)
  - how to design the navigation menu (e.g. categories, search bar, etc.)

#### 1. Structure and Layout

- There is a landing page accessible via main buttons, images, or the footer.
- The top right contains a navigation menu providing access to all pages.
- Considering replacing the account icon with a shopping cart icon for easier access.
- The menu includes all important functions, which seems more logical.

#### 2. Main Compartments and Navigation

- A large, compressed main compartment is displayed at the top of the design.
- This compartment should have a clear structure.
- Includes a section with profile information or image, with an explanation of the design choice.
- Two buttons (white, outlined boxes) lead to the two main compartments:
  - "Ready-to-Buy"
  - "Blueprints"

#### 3. Additional Content and Sections

- Another compartment shows colorful images linking to different subpages:
  - Examples: Products (Ready-to-Buy), Blueprints
  - Each image is visually appealing and links further.
- Below the images, there is a small, transparent text compartment with explanatory text.
- An additional compartment displays images of the company:
  - Images are generated by AI (e.g., company, production, employees).
  - These images link to various pages like "About Us."

#### 4. Further Considerations

- Possibly product detail pages with expandable animations.
- The footer remains constant and contains essential links.
- The design aims to provide a clear structure:
  - Navigation via buttons, images, or the footer.
  - Access through the top-right menu.

#### 5. Functional Ideas

- Replacing the account with a shopping cart for better accessibility.
- Allowing users to customize product colors (e.g., in "Ready-to-Buy").
- Additional functionalities to be added later:
  - Shopping cart
  - Checkout
  - Profiles
  - Other shop features

#### 6. Technical and Design Details

- Images will be generated via AI.
- Transparent areas should blend in subtly.
- Interactive elements like expandable product details and animations are planned.

#### 7. Summary of the Goals

- Create a clear, straightforward structure for the website/app.
- Users should navigate intuitively via buttons, images, and footer.
- Suggestion to switch from account to shopping cart for better accessibility.
- Focus on product customization and appealing design with images and animations.
